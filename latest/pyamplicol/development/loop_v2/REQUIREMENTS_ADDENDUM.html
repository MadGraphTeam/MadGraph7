Loop engine v2 — binding user requirements addendum (2026-10-20)¶

These requirements come directly from the user after reviewing the v2 design. They override the design document (DESIGN.md) where they differ. They apply unchanged to the loop-induced processes added in version 1.0.3 (served at full colour, with the pole selections "all" and "none").

A. Freedom in structure and algorithms; performance gates unchanged¶

The structure and algorithms anticipated in MASTER_PLAN and in the design may be changed freely (data structures, schedules, reduction strategies, rescue tiers, anything internal), provided the public API below is honoured:

  • colour correlations and spin correlations;

  • per-coupling-order partial evaluations (every squared coupling order separately accessible) — this must ALSO be provided at tree level (extend the tree API);

  • leading colour (LC), next-to-leading colour (NLC) and full colour;

  • stability tests (section C).

This freedom concerns HOW the results are computed. It does NOT relax any acceptance requirement. In particular the performance acceptance gates of MASTER_PLAN §5.8 still hold in full, for a normal evaluation that can be compared with other tools (MadLoop, Recola, OpenLoops2, …): faster than MadLoop, and at most 1.30x each available matched Recola/OpenLoops reference (section D gives the comparison protocol). Correctness requirements are likewise unchanged.

Performance priorities (user, 2026-10-21):

  • What matters is 2->3 and higher multiplicities. The gates apply to processes with at least five external legs (2->3 and beyond); 2->2 timings are reported but carry no gate, and being faster than MadLoop at 2->2 is not a goal.

  • The aspiration at 2->3 and beyond is to be the fastest of all tools (MadLoop, Recola, OpenLoops2, …). The formal acceptance threshold remains: faster than MadLoop and at most 1.30x each matched Recola/OpenLoops reference.

  • Optimisation effort should therefore target the asymptotic, high-multiplicity regime first (the required 2->5 set and the 2->3/2->4 ladder below it).

Consequences: lifting/integrand-sum reduction, hybrid rescue tiers, schedule choices, etc. may be adopted whenever measured faster at equal correctness.

B. Poles are optional at runtime¶

  • The Born-virtual result exposes the finite part and the 1/eps and 1/eps^2 coefficients, but pole computation must be switchable OFF at runtime (finite-only mode) and ON on request, for the same generated artifact.

  • Exploit every saving available in finite-only mode (e.g. skip pole-only epsilon sectors, pole-only counterterm columns, pole parts of masters, the IR-pole consistency check). If an efficient hybrid is too complicated, two independent interpretation/execution lanes (finite-only vs with-poles programs) inside one artifact are acceptable.

C. Stability estimates are returned by the API; escalation is the user’s job¶

  • Evaluations return, alongside the values, an estimate of numerical stability (relative accuracy) measured with MadLoop-style checks:

    1. Rotation checks: re-evaluate with Lorentz-rotated external kinematics. The user chooses which rotations (axes/angles or rotation specifications) and how many, including 0.

    2. Reduction-direction check: re-reduce the same numerators with the loop direction reversed (or an equivalent relabelled reduction frame), reusing numerator coefficients and cached masters — the cheap check. Both modes must be supported, individually configurable and combinable. “No stability check” must always be possible; then no precision estimate is returned (explicitly absent, not a fake value).

  • The library does not need to escalate precision automatically: the user decides whether to re-evaluate in DoubleFloat/arbitrary precision based on the returned estimate. (Existing automatic rescue may remain as an optional convenience but is not required and must not be on by default for timing.)

D. Speed-gate protocol¶

  • The performance gate (faster than MadLoop; <= 1.30x each available Recola / OpenLoops) is mandatory and is evaluated on the FINITE PART ONLY, for a normal evaluation comparable across tools, for processes with 2->3 and higher multiplicity (2->2 timings are reported without a gate).

  • Reference codes run in their production mode with pole computation disabled whenever they allow it (OpenLoops truepoles 0; Recola without IR-pole evaluation; MadLoop at its production setting), Collier caches as in production.

  • pyAmpliCol is timed in finite-only mode with stability checks disabled.

  • Correctness validation still covers all three Laurent coefficients.

E. Tree level¶

  • Per squared-coupling-order partial results must be accessible at tree level too, through the same public API conventions (Python, CLI, SDKs where applicable).

F. Heavy processes and slow references (user, 2026-10-22, refined)¶

  • All MASTER_PLAN §5.7 2->5 processes, including g g > g g g g g and u u~ > g g g g g, remain pyAmpliCol targets. Push pyAmpliCol’s own generation and validation for them whenever it has a realistic shot at completing generation (estimate the cost first; otherwise report a precise scaling blocker).

  • Do not launch or wait for a reference-provider generation that cannot complete in about 45 minutes or in a time comparable to pyAmpliCol’s own generation of the process. Validate by other means then (Ward/gauge identities, Catani pole structure, LC/NLC/FC and DD/AP consistency, stability checks, lower-multiplicity oracles) and use references that already exist.

  • Measured provider generation on the development host: g g > g g g g g MadLoop

    17 h (colour factors ~89 days by MG5’s estimate), Recola > 57 h unfinished; u u~ > g g g g g MadLoop 12.2 h, Recola 2.8 h (results exist); g g > g g g g MadLoop 2.4 h, Recola 34 min (results exist).

  • Where no matched provider timing is obtainable, the speed gate is reported as such (never extrapolated) and the user decides how it is judged.

G. Portable prepared artifacts (user, 2026-10-22)¶

Artifacts prepared and compiled with SymJIT at -O2 (the default jit_optimization_level) must be portable so that prepared artifacts for heavy processes can be distributed: relocatable to another machine, user and path; no absolute paths or workspace locations needed at load; no load-time regeneration of the process (the full program, including trees, Born and counterterm scalars, is in the artifact); no dependence on the build host’s CPU features (SymJIT applications are stored as IR and recompiled at load, as the tree engine’s storage-v4 plane kernels already are); bitwise-identical binary64 results after relocation with the same native build. A dedicated acceptance test covers this.

H. Generation budget at high multiplicity; checks without a provider timing (user, 2026-10-23)¶

  • For one-loop matrix-element generation at high multiplicity, generation may take up to O(hours) and must stay below 150 GB of memory. This replaces the earlier “g g > g g g g g full colour < 10 min and < 8 GB” target. Generation and runtime must still be optimised as far as possible; the per-point speed gates of section D are unchanged wherever a matched provider timing exists.

  • Order: first complete the planned 2->5 set in full, then push the limit. g g > g g g g g must be pushed through. g g > 6 g (2->6, the stretch target) is to be attempted if judged doable, leading colour first and full colour next.

  • Where no matched provider timing exists (g g > g g g g g), the speed result is reported only, without a gate. It must come with sanity checks: rotational invariance, and Ward identities probed by replacing one external gluon’s polarisation vector by its momentum.

I. UV and IR pole selection (user, 2026-10-23)¶

  • The user’s words: “you can now pin/rebase the latest main of ninjars and oneloopmasters as those extend now to the possibility of specifying to compute just the IR or UV poles of the reduction, which is an option that should be broadcasted to the user-provided settings in pyAmpliCol for the one-loop matrix elements so that the user can also choose to compute / access only the IR or UV poles (not just no poles) if they so desire”.

  • Requirement (extends B): the one-loop matrix elements take a runtime pole option poles = "all" | "none" | "uv" | "ir" for the same generated artifact. "all" stays the default and its results are unchanged; "none" is the finite-only mode of B with all its savings; "uv" and "ir" return only the ultraviolet or only the infrared pole parts. The finite part is identical in all four modes. The option reaches TOML, CLI, the Python API and the result accessors (and the SDKs where they evaluate loop virtuals).

  • Semantics, decided by the user (~19:20 UTC, relayed by the coordinator’s DECISIONS 2026-10-23; binding): “poles=’uv’ should return the UV poles of the bare (unrenormalised) one-loop amplitude, with the counterterms’ UV poles also accessible separately so I can check renormalisation; poles=’ir’ returns the IR (Catani) poles of the renormalised virtual.”

    • "uv": the selected values (VirtualEvaluation.values and the per-order, correlated and resolved outputs) are the UV poles of the bare loop amplitude interfered with the Born, the double pole zero. The counterterms’ UV poles (uv_local, uv_external_fields, their sum uv, and r2) are in pole_parts; bare + counterterm UV poles = 0 per Laurent coefficient where renormalisation is complete.

    • "ir": the IR poles of the renormalised virtual, equal to Catani’s operator.

    • The finite part is identical in all four modes; "all" stays the default and "none" the finite-only mode.

    • Design §12.4 states what is a definition and what is evidence: a UFO model writes the external-field factors with one eps, so their UV part is fixed by the UV finiteness of the renormalised virtual and the bare + counterterm cancellation in pole_parts holds by construction. The independent evidence is the bare-loop UV residue against the generic engine’s UV-selected masters, bare UV + UV_local + the textbook Feynman-gauge field renormalisation of every leg = 0, and the "ir" poles against Catani’s operator.

  • Companion plan (coordinator decision under the user’s relaxed generation budget, DECISIONS 2026-10-23): kept as built, always generated and stored, so one artifact serves every mode. Its generation cost (about 11 % of the slice generation at five legs and 15 % at six, plus export) is paid by every process; its evaluation cost only by "uv" and "ir": +1 % (u u~ > e- e+ g g, six legs with a photon), +7 % (g g > g g g, five legs) and +13–18 % (g g > g g g g, six legs; idle and loaded host) over "all" (design §12.4).

  • As built (WP2.10 after WP2.6, design §12.2 and §12.4): TOML evaluation.poles, CLI --poles and the API poles= take all four values (PoleSelection.UV, .IR); one artifact serves them all (it stores the companion plan of the certified-scaleless families that "uv"/"ir" run; artifacts written before the split are refused at load with a regeneration message, no backward compatibility). VirtualEvaluation.pole_parts holds both origins by component (virtual, bare, uv, r2, uv_local, uv_external_fields), also for correlated and resolved outputs. The SDKs evaluate legacy plan-v3 artifacts only; v4 SDK evaluation (WP3.1) takes the same option.

J. Counterterms as plain tree-level insertions; no loop matching (user, 2026-10-23)¶

  • R2 and UV counterterm vertices, two-point ones included, are ordinary tree-level vertices of the recursion, with the constraint that each contribution contains exactly one counterterm insertion. They are not mapped onto their loop counterparts. MadLoop-style matching of R2/UV to the loop (witness tables of divergent loop routings, admitted alternatives per routing, leg-mask admission, fallbacks to site closures) is not used in production: it is complicated and costly. Site closure and similar machinery may remain only as test oracles.

  • Admission of counterterm terms = the requested coupling orders, plus the exactly-once constraint, plus a particle_veto filter: a coupling term whose loop_particles contain a vetoed particle is dropped.

  • particle_veto is a public generation option (TOML, CLI, Python API; stored in the artifact). A vetoed particle is also removed from every tree-level vertex in all generation steps (Born, blocks, loop content and propagators), so loops and counterterms stay consistent.

  • A counterterm vertex that cannot enter any tree of the request’s counterterm program is inert: it contributes nothing, needs no lowering, and its rule is recorded as inert in the generation record. “Can enter” is decided by the tree engine’s own reachability over the request’s legs, coupling-order limits and particle_veto (the reachability the counterterm program is built with), never by a site or witness list or by loop matching, and it must be sound: a vertex that can enter a tree is never inert. A request admitting a vertex that cannot be lowered and can enter one of its trees is refused (coordinator decision, 2026-10-23 ~23:50 UTC). Implementation note (ct-simplify): the generation record is the process’s generation filter counterterms_inert (rule -> reason) in the artifact’s generation extension, next to particle_veto (DESIGN §6.2).
