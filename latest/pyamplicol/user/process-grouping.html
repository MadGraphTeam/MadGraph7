Process grouping¶

A multiprocess request such as p p > j j j j expands into many subprocesses, but many of them have the same matrix element once their legs are relabelled. In the built-in Standard Model with five massless flavours, the 486 subprocesses of p p > j j j j fall into 23 classes. Within a class, all subprocesses are related by a permutation of quark flavours, and possibly by charge conjugation. pyAmpliCol records such relations in the artifact, can skip generating the redundant subprocesses, can find relations numerically, and exports the skipped subprocesses to UMAMI through their representative.

The sections below define the relation, explain how candidate subprocesses and leg mappings are found, and describe each mechanism, the file format and the UMAMI export. A worked example with p p > j j j j closes the page.

The relation between two subprocesses¶

A group has one representative, which is always a generated subprocess, and any number of members. Each member carries a relation to the representative:

ME_member(p[0], ..., p[n-1]) = factor * ME_representative(q[0], ..., q[n-1])
q[r] = p[permutation[r]]

  • Legs are counted from 0 in the public order of the process expression, incoming legs first. Momenta are four-vectors in that order.

  • permutation[r] is the member leg that plays the role of representative leg r. This is the convention of ProcessAlias.particle_permutation and of the UMAMI momentum permutation.

  • A permutation never moves a leg across >. It may exchange the two incoming legs, as between d g > d g and g u > u g.

  • factor is a signed rational number whose numerator and denominator are at most 99 in absolute value. --flavor-blind records factor 1; a flavour-blind member gets another factor only when its group is later merged numerically. A member that was not generated is evaluated through its representative and must have a positive factor; a map that would give one a non-positive factor is rejected when it is written.

  • conjugated marks a relation that includes charge conjugation (see Charge conjugation).

  • Helicities follow the legs: the member’s helicity on leg permutation[r] equals the representative’s helicity on leg r. This also holds for conjugated relations.

For example, the relation between u u~ > d u d~ u~ and its representative d d~ > d u d~ u~ has permutation [0, 1, 3, 2, 5, 4]:

ME(u u~ > d u d~ u~)(p0, p1, p2, p3, p4, p5)
    = ME(d d~ > d u d~ u~)(p0, p1, p3, p2, p5, p4)

Representative leg 2 (the outgoing d) takes the momentum of member leg 3 (the outgoing u), so the relabelling is u <-> d, applied to every leg.

Particle types¶

A relabelling may only exchange particles that the dynamics cannot tell apart. pyAmpliCol summarises everything a relabelling must preserve in a particle type:

Component

Meaning

spin

2S+1: 1 for scalars, 2 for fermions, 3 for vectors

colour

dimension of the SU(3) representation: 1, 3 or 8 (triplet and antitriplet are not distinguished)

mass

the name of the mass parameter, or ZERO

width

the name of the width parameter, or ZERO

self-conjugate

whether the particle is its own antiparticle

A particle and its antiparticle share one type. Masses and widths are compared by parameter name, after compile-time resolution. A parameter that the model or its restriction fixes to zero at compile time counts as ZERO. Two different parameters with equal numerical values are never identified.

Examples:

  • Built-in SM: d, u, s, c and b share one type (massless fermion triplet), and the top quark has its own (mass_6, width_6).

  • Shipped sm.json, default restriction: d, u, s and c share one type. The b quark (MB) and the top (MT, WT) each have their own.

  • In sm.json, the massless leptons e- and mu- and the three neutrinos all have the same type (massless colour-singlet fermion), while ta- (MTA) has its own. W+ and W- share a type. The photon and the Z do not, because the Z mass and width differ from ZERO.

The sorted list of particle types of each side is the coarse key of a subprocess. Only subprocesses with equal coarse keys are ever compared.

Flavour profiles¶

Equal types are necessary but not sufficient. The relabelling must also be consistent: a flavour must be renamed the same way on every leg where it appears. pyAmpliCol describes each flavour f of a subprocess by its profile, the four counts

profile(f) = (initial particles, initial antiparticles,
              final particles,   final antiparticles)

Self-conjugate particles are counted as particles. Two subprocesses are related by a type-preserving flavour relabelling exactly when they have the same number of incoming legs and the same multiset of (type, profile) pairs.

Why sorting by PDG code fails¶

A related pair that a positional pairing breaks. Compare u u~ > d u d~ u~ with d d~ > d u d~ u~:

Subprocess

profile of u

profile of d

u u~ > d u d~ u~

(1, 1, 1, 1)

(0, 0, 1, 1)

d d~ > d u d~ u~

(0, 0, 1, 1)

(1, 1, 1, 1)

The (type, profile) multisets agree, so the relabelling u <-> d relates the two. Ordering each side by type, then profile, then flavour, and pairing the ordered legs gives the permutation [0, 1, 3, 2, 5, 4] shown above. Sorting each side by signed PDG code instead pairs the incoming u with the incoming d, but the outgoing u with the outgoing u. No relabelling does that, and evaluating the representative at those momenta computes a different function.

An unrelated pair that a type-only comparison merges. d d~ > u u~ and d d~ > d d~ have the same particle types on each side. The first has only the s-channel gluon exchange. The second also has the t-channel exchange and the interference between the two. Their profiles differ: in d d~ > u u~, d has (1, 1, 0, 0) and u has (0, 0, 1, 1), while in d d~ > d d~, d has (1, 1, 1, 1). --flavor-blind therefore never puts them in one class.

When several flavours of a subprocess share a type and a profile (s and c in d d~ > s c s~ c~), each pairing of them is a valid leg mapping. The first mapping pairs them by increasing |PDG|. The numerical analysis tries at most 24 such mappings, and at most 24 conjugated ones.

Charge conjugation¶

Charge conjugation replaces every particle by its antiparticle, which exchanges the particle and antiparticle entries of each profile. With --consider-charge-conjugation (the default), two subprocesses are also related when one matches the conjugate of the other. Examples are u~ g > u~ g g g and d g > d g g g, or d u~ > d u u~ u~ and d u~ > d d d~ u~. The relation is stored with "conjugated": true, and it means:

  • helicity values move with the legs unchanged: no helicity is flipped;

  • leading-colour (LC) flow words are relabelled and reversed, because every quark line runs in the opposite direction.

Charge conjugation with unchanged helicities is a symmetry of vector-like interactions such as QCD and QED. It is not a symmetry of chiral couplings such as those of the W and Z. Relations that also need a helicity flip (CP-type relations) are not pursued. For example, u d~ > W+ a and d u~ > W- a have equal helicity-summed matrix elements by CP, but their helicity components differ, so the numerical analysis never groups them. --flavor-blind does put them in one class, with a warning that a chiral electroweak vertex breaks charge conjugation.

--no-consider-charge-conjugation (process.consider_charge_conjugation = false) disables conjugated relations. The setting only matters together with --flavor-blind or the numerical analysis.

Flavour-blind generation¶

--flavor-blind (process.flavor_blind = true) states an assumption: every subprocess in a flavour class has the same matrix element as the class representative. A flavour class contains the subprocesses related by a type-preserving flavour relabelling and, unless --no-consider-charge-conjugation, by charge conjugation.

Generation then proceeds as follows:

  1. The request is expanded and checked exactly as without the flag. Subprocesses are named at this stage, so a generated subprocess has the same process ID with or without --flavor-blind.

  2. The expanded subprocesses are partitioned into flavour classes. The representative is the canonical member: it has the smallest |PDG| codes, and particles come before antiparticles. For example, d d~ > g g represents u u~ > g g, and d g > d g represents u~ g > u~ g.

  3. Only representatives are generated. Each other member is recorded in process-groups.json with factor 1, basis flavor-blind, "generated": false, and the leg permutation of the first flavour mapping. A non-conjugated mapping is preferred; a conjugated one is used only when no non-conjugated mapping exists.

A subprocess with an explicit ProcessAlias keeps its own artifact entry and is not folded into a class.

Generation logs the outcome of step 3 in one line, such as Flavor-blind grouping: 486 subprocesses -> 23 generated representatives; 463 routed members (140 by charge conjugation); 0 relabelling warnings, and the summary table printed at the end has a process grouping row; see the worked example.

Members stay selectable. Every SDK (Runtime.load(..., process=...), pyamplicol evaluate --process, rusticol_runtime_load and the C++, Fortran and Rust wrappers) resolves a member ID or expression, in any side order, through process-groups.json to its representative. The representative is evaluated at the permuted momenta and every output is multiplied by the recorded factor. Helicity and colour-flow IDs are reported in the member’s leg order; for a conjugated member each LC flow word is reversed, and colour-flow selectors given in member IDs are translated back. See Process-group members.

from pyamplicol import Runtime

runtime = Runtime.load("artifacts/pp_4j_blind", process="d u~ > d u u~ u~")
runtime.representative_process_key  # the generated representative
runtime.process_group_factor  # Fraction(1, 1)
runtime.charge_conjugated  # True for this member
values = runtime.evaluate(points)  # points in the member's leg order

The UMAMI export applies this mapping automatically (see UMAMI export).

Warnings¶

The assumption is not verified. Generation checks whether each relation is a symmetry of the model vertices that the representative is generated with. A vertex is allowed when its coupling orders fit max_coupling_orders and, under the default minimal coupling-order policy, the representative’s minimal coupling-order envelope. The check works as follows:

  • The relabelling is completed to a permutation of the model’s particles. Flavours that the relabelling moves but that occur in only one of the two subprocesses are paired by particle type. Every allowed vertex that the permutation moves must have an allowed image with the same particles in the same order and the same structure and coupling. For compiled (UFO or JSON) models, structure and coupling mean the Lorentz and colour expressions and the resolved coupling. For the built-in SM, they mean the vertex kind and its coupling.

  • A conjugated relation also requires that the conjugate of every allowed vertex is allowed, and that no allowed vertex couples fermions chirally. A Lorentz structure is chiral when exchanging its left- and right-handed projectors changes it, or when it contains gamma5. A built-in electroweak current is chiral when its left- and right-handed couplings differ.

A member that fails the check gets a warning, stored as its warning field in process-groups.json. The warnings are also logged once on the pyamplicol.generation logger, with their count and up to three examples. For pyamplicol generate "p p > z j" OUT --model built-in-sm --flavor-blind:

'u u~ > g z' assumed equal to 'd d~ > g z', but relabelling d->u, u->d changes
the allowed vertex [d a d] (coupling orders: QCD<=1, QED<=1)

'u~ g > u~ z' assumed equal to 'd g > d z', but relabelling d->u, u->d changes
the allowed vertex [d a d] and charge conjugation is broken by the chiral
allowed vertex [d z d] (coupling orders: QCD<=1, QED<=1)

The check inspects vertices, not diagrams, and completes the relabelling in one fixed way, so it can warn about a relation that holds. In the same run, s s~ > g z is equal to d d~ > g z at tree level, but it still warns: d <-> s alone moves the allowed W vertex [d w+ u], although also exchanging u <-> c would restore it.

Strict flavour-blind generation¶

--flavor-blind-strict (process.flavor_blind_strict = true, which implies flavor_blind) routes only the relations that pass the check above. Within a flavour class, each member tries every flavour pairing, conjugated ones last, against each representative kept so far, and joins the first one without a warning. A member that matches none is generated and becomes a representative of its own, which later members may join. No member of the written map has a warning. For p p > j j with explicit electroweak orders, u u~ > g g is then generated next to d d~ > g g, while s c > s c still joins d u > d u.

The check is local and conservative, so strict generation may generate a member whose relation holds; it never routes a member the check rejects.

When to use it¶

Equal matrix elements within a class are your responsibility. The flag is safe when the interactions that can appear are blind to the relabelled flavours. The typical case is pure-QCD jets with massless quarks under the default minimal coupling-order policy: p p > j j, p p > j j j and p p > j j j j produce no warnings.

It is not safe when photons, Z or W bosons can couple to the relabelled flavours:

  • p p > z j, p p > a j, p p > e+ e- j: up- and down-type quarks have different electric charges and Z couplings, and the chiral Z and W couplings break charge conjugation;

  • jets with electroweak orders allowed explicitly, for example --coupling-order-policy explicit --max-coupling-order QED=2;

  • requests that mix charged leptons and neutrinos: in massless-lepton models they share a type, so p p > e+ e- and p p > ve ve~ would fall into one class.

To check an assumption, generate the subprocesses in question without --flavor-blind and run the numerical analysis: it merges exactly the relations that hold. For example, with u u~ > a a and d d~ > a a it finds ME(u u~ > a a) = 16 ME(d d~ > a a), the ratio (Q_u / Q_d)^4 of the quark charges. --flavor-blind would have assumed factor 1.

Conserved charges in UFO expansion¶

When generating with a UFO or other compiled model, a multiparticle request is expanded in four steps:

  1. Each side of > is filled as a multiset of particles, so two candidates never differ only by an ordering within one side.

  2. Candidates that violate an additive charge conserved by every vertex are dropped.

  3. The colour-readiness probe runs on the remaining candidates.

  4. Tree amplitudes are constructed. With --flavor-blind, the flavour classes are formed just before this step.

A conserved charge assigns a rational value to every particle, the opposite value to its antiparticle and zero to self-conjugate particles. The values on every vertex term sum to zero. The conserved charges are a basis of the rational nullspace of the vertex/particle incidence matrix, computed from the model’s vertex list alone. No quantum numbers are hard-coded.

Every vertex conserves these charges, so every tree graph does too. A candidate whose incoming and outgoing totals differ can have no tree amplitude, whatever the coupling orders. The filter therefore removes nothing that amplitude construction would have kept; it only drops early the candidates that would have failed later. A request with no candidate left is rejected with an error. For the shipped sm.json with its default restriction, the basis has nine charges, spanning electric charge, one quark number per generation (the CKM matrix is diagonal), one lepton number per generation and two ghost numbers. With a non-diagonal CKM matrix, the three quark numbers reduce to baryon number.

The built-in SM keeps its own catalogue prefilters (flavor_scheme, max_quark_lines). pyamplicol model processes applies the same charge filter, but not the colour and amplitude checks. See Models and Processes for the expansion syntax.

Numerical analysis¶

--analyze-process-groups numerical (process.analyze_process_groups = "numerical") runs after the artifact is written. pyamplicol group-processes runs the same analysis on an existing artifact. The analysis looks for relations among the generated group representatives and merges the groups it relates. Flavour-blind members are not analysed: they have no runtime process of their own, and loading one only evaluates its representative.

Procedure¶

  1. Representatives are visited in canonical order. Each one is compared with the earlier representatives that kept their own group and have the same coarse key and the same colour accuracy.

  2. The candidate leg mappings are, in order: the flavour-profile mappings, then (with charge conjugation) the conjugated profile mappings, then a fallback that pairs the legs of each side by type and signed PDG code.

  3. Three phase-space points are used, in f64. At each point, the model inputs are varied independently and deterministically. Each mutable parameter of kind external or normalization, other than a particle mass or width, is multiplied by a factor in [0.9, 1.1]. The factor depends only on the parameter name, the point and the seed, so both processes of a comparison always see the same values. Couplings and other derived parameters follow from the varied inputs. Masses and widths are never varied. A derived mass, such as MW computed from Gf, aEWM1 and MZ in sm.json, is read back from the runtime after the variation.

  4. Each point is on shell for the resolved masses. It is generated in the centre-of-mass frame with the beams along the z axis (massive beams are supported), at sqrt(s) = max(1000, 2 * threshold) in the model’s energy unit. The threshold is the larger of the sum of the beam masses and the sum of the final-state masses. The final state is a massive RAMBO point. A 2 > 1 process uses sqrt(s) equal to the final mass, and a decay is evaluated at rest.

  5. The representative is evaluated at its points, and the candidate member at the permuted points p[permutation[r]] = q[r]. All surviving mappings are evaluated in one batch per point.

  6. Every resolved component is compared through the mapping. At LC, a component is a helicity configuration times a colour flow. Helicity values are permuted. Flow words are relabelled, and reversed for a conjugated mapping; they are compared as sets of open colour lines, or as one adjoint trace up to cyclic rotation. At NLC and full colour, a component is a helicity configuration times a contracted colour component.

A mapping is accepted when one common ratio member / representative describes every component at all three points:

Quantity

Rule

zero components

below 1e-12 times the largest component at that point; a zero on one side must be a zero on the other

agreement of a non-zero component

relative 1e-9, never tighter than 1e-9 * 1e-4 times the largest component

common ratio across points

relative 1e-9

a point that vanishes entirely on either side

no relation

imaginary part of the ratio

at most 1e-9 of its modulus

recorded factor

rational within 1e-9 relative, numerator and denominator at most 99 in absolute value

--only-unit-multiplier

only factor 1 is accepted

seed

20260917, recorded in the map

The search stops at the first earlier group and mapping that pass. The subprocess’s group is then merged into that group with basis numerical.

A representative is skipped, and stays its own group, in any of these cases:

  • it was generated with selected helicities (incomplete helicity coverage);

  • it was generated with selected colour flows or sectors;

  • its masses leave no on-shell phase space;

  • its runtime raises an error.

Each skip is logged as a warning and listed in the summary.

on

also try conjugated mappings

The numerical default belongs to the subcommand, and it takes precedence over a --card value. Pass --analyze-process-groups none or --set process.analyze_process_groups=none to change it. The schema default of process.analyze_process_groups, used by generation, remains none.

The command reads the stored map, analyses its representatives, merges the new relations, and rewrites process-groups.json in place. The rewrite runs under the lock that also serializes generation and appends. The file’s payload record in artifact.json is updated, but the artifact ID is unchanged, because the process-groups role is not part of the runtime identity. UMAMI files are never touched. The command prints a summary; add --json for machine-readable output. For the five subprocesses of the file example, generated without analysis:

representative is the group the subprocess joined. A second run reports groups_before: 2, processes_analysed: 2 and no new relation.

When two groups merge, the canonically smaller representative stays. Every member of the absorbed group, including its flavour-blind members, is re-expressed through the new link:

  • factors multiply;

  • permutations compose;

  • the conjugated flags combine (two conjugations cancel);

  • the basis becomes the least verified of the two links, in the order identity, numerical, flavor-blind;

  • a warning is kept.

The analysis settings are appended to analyses unless an identical entry is already there. Rerunning with the same settings is deterministic and adds nothing. A later run with relaxed settings, for example without --only-unit-multiplier, can add relations.

The Python equivalent is pyamplicol.group_processes (also available as pyamplicol.api.group_processes). It takes the same options as keyword arguments: analysis="numerical", only_unit_multiplier=False and consider_charge_conjugation=True. See Python API.

The process-groups.json file¶

Every generation lane (compiled, eager, recurrence, on-the-fly) writes process-groups.json at the artifact root, declared in artifact.json with payload role process-groups and media type application/json. Without --flavor-blind and without numerical analysis, each generated subprocess is its own group with factor 1. An append keeps the stored groups and adds the new subprocesses. A newly generated subprocess whose process ID matches an ungenerated flavour-blind member replaces that member and becomes its own group.

The example below comes from

pyamplicol generate "d d~ > a a" artifacts/grouping_demo --model built-in-sm \
  --process "u u~ > a a" --process "d g > d g" --process "d~ g > d~ g" \
  --process "g u > u g" --analyze-process-groups numerical

UMAMI export of flavour-blind members¶

The UMAMI provider exports every flavour-blind member, although it has no runtime process:

  • A member adds flavour selectors that evaluate its representative’s runtime, with no runtime_processes row of its own. Its members[].runtime names the representative.

  • The momentum permutation is the composition of the recorded relation with the runtime ordering. It may exchange the incoming legs, but the selector keeps the member’s physical incoming PDG codes for the PDF weights, so this is not a beam-exchange assumption.

  • A conjugated member uses the representative’s colour flows reversed. Its LHE colour tags and helicity labels are those of the member.

  • The member’s factor (1 for flavour-blind relations) multiplies the runtime factor.

  • Every provider exporting such members records an additional grouping.assumptions entry:

    flavor-blind matrix elements: ungenerated process-group members are
evaluated through their relabelled representative (assumed, not proven)

A member whose relabelling moves unequal masses between legs, or maps a restricted helicity selection onto a different set, is exported by a separate provider that loads the representative’s runtime. Warnings stay in process-groups.json; they are not copied into the UMAMI metadata. Numerically grouped subprocesses are generated and keep their ordinary export; group-processes does not change the UMAMI files. See UMAMI and MadSpace for the selector layout.

Limitations¶

  • --flavor-blind is an assumption. Its warning comes from a per-vertex check and can be raised for a relation that holds (see Warnings).

  • A flavour-blind representative that later turns out to have no tree-level amplitude is skipped as unsupported, and its members disappear with it. The skip warning gives only their number, not their names.

  • A routed member uses its representative’s model parameters, so its value follows the representative’s flavours. Changing a parameter that distinguishes their flavours (a mass, width or Yukawa coupling) invalidates the relation; generate the process explicitly if it must follow independent parameter values.

  • Correlated evaluation through a member is supported only when the composed permutation (relation and requested order) is the identity and the relation is not conjugated; the factor is then applied. Other selections fail with an error naming the member and its representative.

  • For conjugated members with several open quark lines, the reversed LC flow IDs may list the open lines in another order than a separate generation of the same subprocess would; values and flow selection agree.

  • The numerical analysis is a test at three f64 points, not a proof: two matrix elements that agree at those points within 1e-9 are grouped.

  • The search covers only relations with a rational factor whose numerator and denominator are at most 99, a leg permutation within each side, and optional charge conjugation with unchanged helicities. It does not cover CP-type relations or crossing between the initial and final states.

  • Subprocesses generated with selected helicities or colour flows are never grouped numerically.

  • Relations are only searched between subprocesses with equal particle types per side, so two different mass parameters with equal values are never identified.

  • Numerical relations do not shrink the artifact or the UMAMI export: both subprocesses stay generated.
