\section{Standard-Model Process Matrices}
\label{sec:three-mode-performance-tables}

The process matrices use recurrence as the primary \PAC{} execution mode.
Built-in-SM and UFO-SM recurrence JIT O2 are compared with independent
original-\AC{} results.  Separate built-in-SM matrices compare compiled JIT O3
and eager JIT O2 with recurrence JIT O2 for the same process, colour treatment,
and observable. A built-in-SM on-the-fly JIT O2 matrix compares directly with
original-\AC{} for LC at \(n_{\mathrm{final}}\leq4\).

The canonical matrix spans electroweak vector production, charged-current and
leptonic channels, heavy-quark production, pure gluons, multi-boson final
states, Higgs-associated final states, and three- and four-quark-line cases.
Here \(n\) is the number of final-state particles.  LC coverage retains every
declared family through \(n=9\), except that on-the-fly coverage currently ends
at \(n_{\mathrm{final}}=4\). NLC and full-colour grids display
\(n=1,\ldots,6\). Their
on-the-fly tables are explicit future placeholders and contain no runnable
on-the-fly cells. A multiplicity for which a process family does not yet exist
is marked \textsc{not applicable}; an otherwise defined process intentionally
excluded from the declared measurement scope is marked \textsc{not run}.
These are table structure, not unfilled measurements.  A complete edition
accounts for every scheduled cell across the full declared range.
Resource-limited cells carry a documented status; every successful cell is
numerical and validated.  An in-scope result that does not pass numerical
validation is identified by a status and does not carry a timing ratio.
Best-mode cells select the fastest successful candidate, including on-the-fly
where it is in scope and marking that winner with \texttt{(o)}. If none
succeeds, the recurrence attempt is shown with \texttt{(r)} and its terminal
status.

The UFO-SM recurrence matrices use the same process definitions, colour
contracts, numerical parameters, selectors, and validation points as the
built-in-SM recurrence matrices. Their source is the packaged Standard Model
loaded through the serialized-JSON interface. This comparison verifies that the
imported and built-in model routes implement the same requested physics.
Compiled, eager, and on-the-fly process matrices are built-in-only, isolating
differences between execution modes from differences in model loading.

\subsection*{Workloads and denominators}

For a reported timing quantity \(T\), each multiplier is
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{candidate/baseline}}
  = \frac{T_{\mathrm{candidate}}}{T_{\mathrm{baseline}}}.
  \label{eq:three-mode-matrix-time-ratio}
\end{equation}
Green, orange, and red denote respectively \(r<1\), \(1\leq r<2\), and
\(r\geq2\).  Green therefore means that the candidate is faster than the
baseline named in the table heading.  The recurrence matrices use original
\AC{} as the baseline, as does the detailed on-the-fly LC matrix. The compiled
and eager matrices use built-in-SM recurrence JIT O2 as the baseline.

Every LC comparison contains two complete-coverage layouts:
\begin{itemize}
  \item topology replay, measured with one physical colour flow selected and
        all retained helicities summed; and
  \item the all-flow union, measured with one physical helicity selected and
        all retained colour flows summed.
\end{itemize}
Neither selection is fixed during generation. Each LC cell reports the two
baseline generation quantities and wall times, followed by candidate
generation entries and runtime ratios. The muted supplementary ratio first
compares separately measured execution attributions when both sides expose
compatible boundaries; otherwise it compares authenticated evaluator totals.
Only against original \AC{}, which has no evaluator-total clock, may a candidate
evaluator total use the legacy direct execution clock (or wall when direct
execution is absent) as denominator. An evaluator total is never divided by a
recurrence core. This evaluator-total fallback is diagnostic only;
execution/execution remains the compatible supplementary attribution. If no
compatible supplementary clock exists, only the wall ratio is shown. Compiled
and eager generation are compared with recurrence using the same layout.

On-the-fly is the scoped LC exception to the two-artifact layout. One compact
process-seed artifact serves both the selected-flow, helicity-summed
workload and the all-flow, single-helicity workload. Its process-generation
time is \(G\). For selected flow, \texttt{([xG] x(G+W))} shows the muted
generation-only multiplier and the normally coloured multiplier after adding
the first full-batch evaluation \(W\), both relative to the matching \AC{}
library-generation time. For all flow, \texttt{[G] G+W} shows the two absolute
times, with \(G\) muted and \(G+W\) in normal black. In this generation-only
notation, \(W\) excludes artifact loading and the conventional benchmark
warmup calls.

The two original-\AC{} reference workloads have different setup boundaries.
For the selected-flow workload, the reported generation quantity is creation
of the process library. For the all-flow workload, it is preparation of direct
all-flow evaluation. Runtime ratios compare the same physical observable. The
all-flow setup quantities are not like-for-like, so the table shows the
candidate process-generation time as an absolute value and forms no ratio.
For on-the-fly this is the absolute \texttt{[G] G+W} pair above.

NLC and full colour use one helicity-summed, colour-contracted calculation per
mode. Their cells report the absolute baseline generation and wall time,
followed by the candidate generation and runtime ratios. No per-flow scalar is
defined for these contracted colour accuracies. On-the-fly does not yet expose
these colour treatments, so its NLC and full-colour tables are static future
placeholders rather than missing measurements.

The wall quantity is the repeated native evaluation time after caller-language
input conversion and is the primary runtime observable. Independent accumulated
evaluator-total and narrower recurrence-core clocks remain in the detailed
measurement evidence; neither is copied from wall time or from the other. The
compact runtime notation \texttt{([xS]xW)} shows a compatible supplementary
clock multiplier \texttt{xS} in muted brackets and the primary wall multiplier
\texttt{xW} in its performance colour. The hierarchy above determines whether
\texttt{xS} is execution attribution or a diagnostic evaluator-total fallback.
If no supplementary ratio is eligible, the cell shows only \texttt{(xW)}.

Preparation of reusable model-level kernels is excluded from
process-generation time. Compiled generation still includes process-specific
evaluator construction and backend compilation. On-the-fly \(W\) starts only
after the compact artifact has been loaded and stops after its first complete
batch evaluation; later benchmark warmups are separate.

A timing is shown only after recurrence agrees with original \AC{} at the
independent-reference tolerance and compiled and eager results agree with
recurrence at the stricter cross-mode tolerance. Built-in and UFO-SM recurrence
results are also compared directly at that stricter tolerance, rather than
relying on their separate agreement with \AC{}. Each result must reproduce its
resolved sum. For LC, the common flow--helicity component is compared directly
between topology replay and all-flow union, and every \PAC{} all-flow result is
also compared with the matching independently generated \AC{} component. A
failed validation has no timing ratio. On-the-fly adds a recurrence-authority
gate: for both workloads, its complete resolved-component array must agree with
the separately generated recurrence result before timing. After
\texttt{Runtime.clear()}, the full component comparison and the
resolved-sum identity are repeated before the cell is accepted.

\subsection*{Cell and summary layout}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.95in}L{2.45in}L{5.75in}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{contract} & \textbf{baseline entry} & \textbf{candidate entries} \\
\midrule
LC, upper
  & topology replay \texttt{|} all-flow generation quantities in seconds
  & layout-matched ratios, except that the all-flow candidate against original
    \AC{} is shown absolutely without a ratio; on-the-fly uses selected
    \texttt{([xG] x(G+W))} \texttt{|} all-flow \texttt{[G] G+W} \\
LC, lower
  & selected-flow \texttt{|} all-flow wall times in
    \(\mu\mathrm{s}/\mathrm{pt}\)
  & compact \texttt{([xS]xW)} comparisons, or \texttt{(xW)} when only wall is
    comparable \\
NLC and full-colour, upper
  & contracted generation time in seconds
  & candidate generation ratio \\
NLC and full-colour, lower
  & contracted wall time in \(\mu\mathrm{s}/\mathrm{pt}\)
  & compact \texttt{([xS]xW)} comparison, or \texttt{(xW)} when only wall is
    comparable \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Summary rows contain multipliers only, ordered as minimum, maximum, median,
arithmetic mean, and weighted mean.  The arithmetic mean averages the
individual candidate/baseline multipliers; the weighted mean is the ratio of
sums
\(\sum_i T_{\mathrm{candidate},i}/\sum_i T_{\mathrm{baseline},i}\).
Generation summaries use topology replay for LC and the contracted workload
for NLC and full colour; the non-comparable LC all-flow generation quantity is
omitted. On-the-fly generation summaries use \(G\); \(W\) remains visible in
the per-cell cold-start pair. Runtime summaries use wall time only, with
separate aligned LC topology-replay and all-flow lines. Structurally inapplicable,
resource-limited, unsupported, and validation-failed cells do not contribute.

The first three matrices provide a built-in-SM overview against original
\AC{}.  For each process, multiplicity, colour treatment, and LC workload,
they select the validated pyAmpliCol mode with the smallest wall time.
Selection is independent in every cell: the overview records the measured
winner rather than prescribing one mode for all processes.  Its upper row
reports generation and its lower row reports runtime.  LC pairs use
non-union-flow \texttt{|} union-flow ordering.  Since original-\AC{} all-flow
setup and \PAC{} process generation have different boundaries, the latter is
shown absolutely rather than as a misleading ratio. When on-the-fly is the
winner, the same \texttt{([xG] x(G+W))} and \texttt{[G] G+W} generation
notation applies beside its \texttt{(o)} marker.
