\section{Supported UFO and Serialized-JSON Models}
\label{sec:ufo-support}

The external-model path compiles a supported subset of the UFO model format
rather than interpreting arbitrary model code during amplitude evaluation.  A
trusted UFO Python module or its serialized JSON representation is normalized
to a typed, model-independent representation before current recursion begins.
An interaction is executable only when its tensors can be materialized and
every transformation required for its evaluation can be checked algebraically.

\subsection{Common loading and normalization route}

UFO and JSON inputs enter the same compilation route,
\begin{equation}
  \{\text{trusted UFO module},\text{ serialized JSON}\}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{normalized model}
  \longrightarrow
  \text{executable kernels}.
  \label{eq:ufo-loading-route}
\end{equation}
The UFO form is executable Python and must be obtained from a trusted source.
The JSON form is data-only and is the preferred portable representation for
exchange and archival.  After loading, both forms use the same particle,
parameter, propagator, tensor, and coupling-order definitions.

Model symbols occupy a model-local namespace, allowing two models with
equally named parameters to coexist without identifying those parameters.
The normalized representation contains typed particles, parameters,
propagators, vertex terms, oriented kernels, component orderings, contractions,
and Goldstone relations.  Unsupported spin, colour, or function structures are
rejected before process generation.

\subsection{From a UFO vertex to current kernels}

A UFO vertex is a matrix over colour and Lorentz structures:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal V(1,\ldots,n)
  = \sum_{a,b} g_{ab}\,
    C_a(c_1,\ldots,c_n)\,
    L_b(s_1,p_1;\ldots;s_n,p_n).
  \label{eq:ufo-vertex-matrix}
\end{equation}
Each term retains its coupling-order vector and is normalized to an explicit
component basis.  The supported Lorentz basis includes scalars, momenta,
metrics, Dirac identity and gamma matrices, \(\gamma_5\), chiral projectors,
slashed momenta, and sigma tensors.  All physical axes and component orderings
are explicit.

The supported colour representations are the SU(3) singlet, fundamental,
antifundamental, and adjoint.  Trilinear projection recognizes
\begin{equation}
  1,\qquad
  \delta_i{}^{\bar\jmath},\qquad
  (T^a)_i{}^{\bar\jmath},\qquad
  f^{abc}.
  \label{eq:trilinear-color-basis}
\end{equation}
For a proposed basis tensor \(B_\sigma\), projection succeeds only if
\begin{equation}
  C_a(\boldsymbol c)=\kappa_a B_\sigma(\boldsymbol c)
  \quad\text{for every colour component }\boldsymbol c,
  \label{eq:exact-color-projection}
\end{equation}
with exact scalar \(\kappa_a\).  Numerical resemblance is not sufficient to
establish an executable colour structure.

For a trilinear term, contracting the normalized tensor with two input currents
gives an oriented kernel
\begin{equation}
  K^{A_r}_{r\leftarrow ij}(J_i,J_j)
  =g_{a b}\,\kappa_a
    \sum_{A_i,A_j}
    L_b^{A_r}{}_{A_i A_j}(p_i,p_j,p_r)
    J_i^{A_i}J_j^{A_j},
  \qquad p_r=-(p_i+p_j).
  \label{eq:oriented-kernel}
\end{equation}
Couplings, perturbative orders, colour normalization, and input/output
component orderings remain attached to the kernel.  The propagator is applied
only when the result becomes an internal current.

\subsection{Algebraic verification}

Algebraic verification establishes that an optimized or factorized expression
reconstructs the normalized source tensor.  It does not assert that a model is
unitary, renormalizable, or phenomenologically complete.

Kernel reuse, for example, requires the exact relation
\begin{equation}
  K'(x,y;p_x,p_y)
  =\eta\,K(y,x;p_y,p_x),
  \qquad \eta\in\{-1,+1\},
  \label{eq:kernel-equivalence}
\end{equation}
after canonicalizing component expressions and resolving coupling aliases.
When such a relation cannot be established, both kernels are retained.  By
contrast, a contact interaction that cannot be factorized without changing
its tensor, colour coefficient, coupling, or multiplicity is rejected rather
than approximated.

\subsection{Four-point contact factorization}

Two four-point colour structures have a verified auxiliary-current
factorization.  A literal colour singlet is split directly when its normalized
Lorentz tensor can be reconstructed.  A coloured gauge contact must have the
form
\begin{equation}
  C^{a_1a_2a_3a_4}
  = c\,f^{a_i a_j b}f^{b a_k a_r},
  \label{eq:two-f-color}
\end{equation}
up to exact permutations, with one shared adjoint index \(b\) and scalar
coefficient \(c\).  It is represented by a non-propagating auxiliary current,
\begin{align}
  A^{b}_{\rho\sigma}[i,j]
    &= f^{b a_i a_j}
       \sum_{A_i,A_j}
       P_{\rho\sigma;A_iA_j}
       J_i^{A_i}J_j^{A_j},
       \label{eq:contact-partial}\\
  J_r^{A_r}\big|_{V_4}
    &= \frac{c\,s_{ijkr}}{m_r}
       f^{a_r b a_k}
       \sum_{\rho,\sigma,A_k}
       F^{A_r}{}_{\rho\sigma A_k}
       A^{b}_{\rho\sigma}J_k^{A_k}.
       \label{eq:contact-final}
\end{align}
Here \(s_{ijkr}=\pm1\) is the orientation parity and \(m_r\) removes
indistinguishable assignments of identical inputs.  The original coupling and
its complete perturbative order occur only in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:contact-final}; the auxiliary in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:contact-partial} has unit coupling and no propagator.  The
factorization therefore introduces neither an artificial pole nor duplicated
coupling weight.

The open-axis Lorentz tensor is reconstructed component by component.  This
covers the SU(3) four-vector gauge contact and supported colour-singlet
four-vector contacts; other coloured four-point tensors require a separately
verified intermediate colour channel.

\subsection{Higher-valence colour-singlet contacts}

Supported colour-singlet contacts of valence \(n\geq4\) are represented by a
balanced binary tree.  For input set \(S\),
\begin{equation}
  A_S=\Phi_S(A_{S_L},A_{S_R}),
  \qquad S=S_L\mathbin{\dot\cup}S_R,
  \qquad
  J_r\big|_{V_n}=\frac{g}{m_r}
      \Psi_r(A_{S_L},A_{S_R}),
  \label{eq:contact-tree}
\end{equation}
where internal \(A_S\) are non-propagating, and the physical coupling \(g\)
appears only in the final map.  The tree has \(O(n)\) auxiliary operations and
\(O(\log n)\) depth.

For scalar contacts, \(\Phi_S(x,y)=xy\).  For supported non-scalar or
momentum-dependent singlet contacts, the auxiliary retains the components and
momenta needed to reconstruct the original Lorentz expression, using
\begin{equation}
  p_r=-\sum_{i\ne r}p_i.
  \label{eq:contact-momentum-conservation}
\end{equation}
This is generic in valence only after the colour tensor has been proved to be a
singlet; it is not a general algorithm for coloured higher-point contacts.

\subsection{Colour flow and model symmetries}

Leading-colour singlet subtraction follows from the fundamental Fierz identity
\begin{equation}
  (T^a)_i{}^{\bar\jmath}(T^a)_k{}^{\bar l}
  =T_R\left(
    \delta_i{}^{\bar l}\delta_k{}^{\bar\jmath}
    -\frac{1}{N_c}
     \delta_i{}^{\bar\jmath}\delta_k{}^{\bar l}
  \right).
  \label{eq:fierz}
\end{equation}
It is enabled only when the fundamental-generator colour tensor, fermion
representations, Lorentz kernel, and massless adjoint-vector propagator satisfy
the corresponding exact contracts.

Further recognizers establish vectorlike parity, Yang--Mills cubic and quartic
relations, and local adjoint-current reflection.  A global symmetry is used
only when the reachable interaction sector is closed under the verified
relations.  An additional reachable interaction that does not satisfy them
disables the reuse theorem but does not remove any physical contribution.

\subsection{Propagators, closures, and spin two}

Propagators are taken from the model definition.  For standard scalar, Dirac,
vector, and spin-two particles they have the schematic forms
\begin{align}
  P_0(p)&=\frac{i}{p^2-m^2+i m\Gamma},
  &P_{1/2}(p)&=\frac{i(\gamma^\mu p_\mu+m)}{p^2-m^2+i m\Gamma},
  \label{eq:standard-propagators-a}\\
  P_1^{\mu\nu}(p)&=
    \frac{-i}{p^2-m^2+i m\Gamma}
    \left(g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{p^\mu p^\nu}{m^2}\right),
  &P_2^{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}(p)&=
    \frac{i\,\Pi^{\mu\nu,\rho\sigma}(p)}
         {p^2-m^2+i m\Gamma}.
  \label{eq:standard-propagators-b}
\end{align}
The massless-vector branch uses Feynman gauge without the singular
\(p^\mu p^\nu/m^2\) term.  Spin-two currents use a 16-component ordered tensor
space and the massless de Donder or massive Fierz--Pauli projector.
Non-propagating contact auxiliaries use the identity.

A custom UFO propagator is accepted when its numerator and denominator can be
materialized in the declared component ordering.  It is applied as supplied
and disables optimizations that require a standard propagator.  Goldstone
relations are determined from quantum numbers and exact mass expressions;
ambiguous matches are rejected.

Massless spin two is validated by the scalar-gravity model.  Massive spin two
remains experimental because its broad model and constraint coverage has not
yet been qualified.
