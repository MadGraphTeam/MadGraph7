\section{Standard-Model Process Matrices}
\label{sec:three-mode-performance-tables}

The process matrices use recurrence as the primary \PAC{} execution mode.
Built-in-SM and UFO-SM recurrence JIT O2 are compared directly with the
independent original \AC{} reference.  Separate built-in-SM matrices then
compare compiled JIT O3 and eager-DAG JIT O2 with the matching built-in-SM
recurrence JIT O2 measurement.  Baseline measurements are joined when the
tables are rendered; they are not copied into candidate result records.

The canonical matrix spans electroweak vector production, charged-current and
leptonic channels, heavy-quark production, pure gluons, multi-boson final
states, Higgs-associated final states, and three- and four-quark-line cases.
Here \(n\) is the number of final-state particles.  LC coverage retains the
declared grid through \(n=9\), with the bounded families ending at the
multiplicity stated by the process catalog.  NLC and full-colour grids retain
\(n=1,\ldots,5\).  Cells below a family's minimum physical multiplicity, above
its declared limit, or without a valid measurement are shown explicitly as
\ReportNA{}.

The UFO-SM recurrence matrices use the same process definitions, colour
contracts, numerical parameters, selectors, and validation points as the
built-in-SM recurrence matrices.  Their source is the packaged Standard Model
loaded through the generic UFO/JSON compiler.  This comparison tests
model-generic lowering without changing the requested physics or the original
\AC{} reference.  Compiled and eager process matrices are built-in-only; they
measure execution-strategy differences against recurrence without introducing
a second model-loading comparison.

\subsection*{Workloads and denominators}

For a reported timing quantity \(T\), each multiplier is
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{candidate/baseline}}
  = \frac{T_{\mathrm{candidate}}}{T_{\mathrm{baseline}}}.
  \label{eq:three-mode-matrix-time-ratio}
\end{equation}
Green, orange, and red denote respectively \(r<1\), \(1\leq r<2\), and
\(r\geq2\).  Green therefore means that the candidate is faster than the
baseline named in the table heading.  The recurrence matrices use original
\AC{} as the baseline.  The compiled and eager matrices use built-in-SM
recurrence JIT O2 as the baseline.

Every LC comparison contains two independently generated, complete-coverage
artifacts:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{topology-replay}, profiled with one physical colour flow
        selected at runtime and all retained helicities summed; and
  \item \texttt{all-flow-union}, profiled with one physical helicity selected
        at runtime and all retained colour flows summed.
\end{itemize}
Neither the displayed flow nor the displayed helicity is fixed during
generation.  Each LC cell reports the two absolute baseline generation times
and wall times, followed by layout-matched candidate generation ratios and
\texttt{(wall$\mid$execution)} runtime ratios.  Thus compiled and eager
generation are compared separately with recurrence topology replay and
recurrence all-flow union, rather than against one shared recurrence
generation value.

NLC and full colour use one helicity-summed, colour-contracted artifact per
mode.  Their cells report the absolute baseline generation and wall time,
followed by the candidate generation ratio and
\texttt{(wall$\mid$execution)} runtime ratios.  No per-flow scalar is defined
for these contracted colour accuracies.

The wall quantity is the native Rusticol repeated-evaluation time after
caller-language input conversion.  The execution quantity is the native
mode-specific numerical submetric: compiled stage and amplitude evaluation,
eager invocation-table execution, or direct recurrence-schedule execution.
Both quantities are divided by the same matching baseline wall time because
original \AC{} and cross-mode baselines do not expose an equivalent internal
submetric.

Preparing a reusable model-level kernel or recurrence-template bundle is
excluded from every process-generation entry and retained as separate
provenance.  Process-generation timings begin from the prepared input required
by the selected mode.  Compiled timings still include process-specific
evaluator construction and backend compilation.

Pointwise publication requires recurrence to agree with original \AC{} at the
independent-reference tolerance.  Compiled and eager candidates must agree
with recurrence at the stricter shared-core tolerance.  Each candidate must
also reproduce its own resolved sum; LC comparisons additionally use the same
physical flow and helicity contracts in both layouts.  A failed validation
publishes a diagnostic status but no timing ratio.

\subsection*{Cell and summary layout}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.95in}L{2.45in}L{5.75in}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{contract} & \textbf{baseline entry} & \textbf{candidate entries} \\
\midrule
LC, upper
  & topology-replay/all-flow-union generation times in seconds
  & layout-matched generation ratios \\
LC, lower
  & selected-flow/all-flow wall times in microseconds per point
  & for each workload, \texttt{(wall$\mid$execution)} ratios \\
NLC/full, upper
  & contracted generation time in seconds
  & candidate generation ratio \\
NLC/full, lower
  & contracted wall time in microseconds per point
  & \texttt{(wall$\mid$execution)} ratios \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Summary rows report the baseline mean over valid candidate--baseline pairs and
the ratio of sums
\(\sum_i T_{\mathrm{candidate},i}/\sum_i T_{\mathrm{baseline},i}\).
LC summaries remain separate for topology replay and all-flow union.  Missing,
unsupported, resource-limited, and validation-failed cells do not contribute
to a ratio-of-sums summary.
