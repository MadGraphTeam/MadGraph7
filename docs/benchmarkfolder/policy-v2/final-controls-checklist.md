# Final numerical-control checklist

This checklist applies after the final generator and numerical archives are
chosen. Earlier controls and representative runs remain historical evidence;
they do not certify a later source tree or archive. Each completed item must
point to retained commands, source/library identities, cards, point streams,
full output rows and a recomputed comparison.

- [ ] Regenerate the candidate after the `-fno-automatic` classifier fix and
  any subsequently accepted optimization.
- [ ] Refresh role-aware worker/fresh-process parity for the final candidate
  generator and exact reduction-runtime signatures. Reuse unchanged original
  and corrected-original evidence only through explicit validated bindings.
- [ ] Compare loop-induced `gg → H` and `gg → HH` across all four corrected
  candidate backends and the applicable corrected-original references.
- [ ] Compare mixed QCD/QED split orders, including every order and total.
- [ ] Compare complex-mass inputs with the final generated model helpers.
- [ ] Exercise genuine higher precision with CutTools, Ninja and NinjaRS,
  retaining every point and status. The strict ordinary `1e-20` Ninja failures
  in all three source roles stay visible; they are not acceptance evidence.
- [ ] Rerun near-singular points and rotations, including corresponding
  unrotated inputs and independent higher-precision/analytic pole checks.
- [ ] Rerun changed masses/scales, direction reversal, and fresh versus reused
  contexts to verify invalidation and absence of stale results.
- [ ] Verify binary128 inputs distinguishable beyond double precision and
  higher-precision convergence.
- [ ] Refresh standalone/NLO, copied-dependency, installation failure/retry,
  unavailable-library, return-code and statistics-reader controls when their
  implementation or installed artifacts change.
- [ ] Finish all eight complete core streams, corrected-reference comparisons,
  literal original-Ninja timing comparisons and required confidence bounds.

The representative protocol validates repeated execution and transport. It
does not replace cross-backend correctness, exceptional-input controls, or
the per-process five-percent performance requirement.
